# Manual Commercial Playbook

## Review a new prospect

1. Open the company evidence and FirstCustomerFit breakdown.
2. Separate observations from inferences. A public contact form is observed; manual routing is only a hypothesis.
3. Select one public evidence item and one narrow workflow to test.
4. Check first-customer risks: proprietary core system, internal product/engineering capacity, enterprise procurement, unclear local ownership, or no reachable buyer.
5. Resolve a buyer before contact and save only the minimum useful data: name, role, public profile URL, and channel.
6. Draft manually, verify every claim against the selected evidence, and keep a LinkedIn connection note at or below 190 characters.
7. Send outside the system. Then use `/commercial` to record what was actually sent.

## When to reject

Reject or skip when the company is clearly too technologically mature for a first implementation, has proprietary core technology that raises delivery expectations, requires enterprise procurement, is outside the initial local/small-firm motion, has no credible buyer path after a reasonable manual check, or lacks enough public evidence for a responsible opening.

Record the actual reason. Do not tune FirstCustomerFit to justify a decision already made.

## How to choose the buyer

Prefer the person who owns the workflow and can authorize a small operational change:

1. For a small local despacho: owner, managing partner, socio director, or office director.
2. For a larger firm: operations leader or the director responsible for the specific service line.
3. Prefer a local office decision-maker over headquarters when the workflow and budget are local.
4. Do not assume a visible founder handles operations. Validate the role from a public source.
5. If only a role hypothesis exists, keep the name and profile blank and record `buyer_unresolved` or `no_linkedin_found`.

## After acceptance

Use the post-acceptance template as a structure, replace every placeholder, and send manually. Mention the public observation, explicitly avoid assuming an internal problem, and ask how the workflow works today. Do not lead with a product, AI, or a proposed build.

If there is no reply, follow up once when appropriate, then record `no_response`. Do not automate a sequence.

## Three core discovery questions

1. “¿Cómo entra hoy una solicitud o documento de este tipo y quién decide a qué persona o área va?”
2. “¿En qué paso aparece más seguimiento, información que falta, copia manual o espera?”
3. “¿Qué tendría que mejorar —tiempo, errores, capacidad o visibilidad— para que mereciera la pena cambiar ese paso?”

## What counts as validated pain

Validated pain requires a buyer or workflow owner to describe a real current process and at least one concrete consequence: repeated staff time, missed or delayed follow-up, rework, avoidable errors, backlog, capacity constraint, or lack of status visibility. Capture the workflow, frequency or volume when known, current workaround, affected role, consequence, and the buyer’s priority.

A website signal, a FirstCustomerFit score, or Sergio’s inference alone is not validated pain.

## What not to pitch early

- A broad “AI transformation.”
- A chatbot without a validated workflow.
- A CRM or ERP replacement.
- A multi-month platform build.
- Automated outbound messaging.
- A solution before the buyer confirms the process and consequence.

The first useful offer is a small, reversible workflow improvement with a clear before/after measure.
